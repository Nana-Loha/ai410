DIABETES MELLITUS — A1C MANAGEMENT AND INDIVIDUALIZATION GUIDELINE
Source: ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes 2023 (Diabetes Care 2023;46(Suppl 1)),
        AACE/ACE Comprehensive Diabetes Management Algorithm 2022,
        EASD/ADA Consensus Report on Management of Hyperglycemia in T2D 2022
Last Updated: 2023

=============================================================
SECTION 1: A1C TARGETS AND INDIVIDUALIZATION
=============================================================

Standard A1C Target: < 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) for most non-pregnant adults

Individualized Targets — More Stringent (< 6.5%):
- Short duration of diabetes (< 5 years)
- Long life expectancy
- No significant CVD
- No hypoglycemia history
- High patient motivation and self-management capability
- Managed with lifestyle ± metformin only (low hypoglycemia risk)

Individualized Targets — Less Stringent (< 8.0%):
- History of severe hypoglycemia
- Limited life expectancy (< 5-10 years)
- Advanced microvascular or macrovascular complications
- Extensive comorbidities
- Longstanding diabetes with difficulty achieving target despite multiple agents
- Frail elderly or cognitive impairment
- High hypoglycemia unawareness

Key Principle: "Treat to target, not to failure"
- Any A1C reduction is beneficial
- Avoid therapeutic inertia (failure to intensify regimen when A1C above target)
- Reassess target every visit; adjust as patient circumstances change

=============================================================
SECTION 2: MONITORING PARAMETERS
=============================================================

A1C Testing Frequency:
- Every 3 months: If not at goal OR after any treatment change
- Every 6 months: If stable and at goal

A1C Limitations and Conditions Affecting Accuracy:
- Falsely HIGH: Iron deficiency anemia, vitamin B12 deficiency, splenectomy
- Falsely LOW: Hemolytic anemia, blood transfusion, hemoglobinopathies (HbS, HbC)
- Alternative monitoring in these situations: Fructosamine or glycated albumin

Time in Range (TIR) — CGM Metrics:
- TIR (70-180 mg/dL): Target ≥ 70% (corresponds approximately to A1C < 7%)
- TBR (< 70 mg/dL): Target < 4%
- TBR (< 54 mg/dL): Target < 1%
- TAR (> 180 mg/dL): Target < 25%
- TAR (> 250 mg/dL): Target < 5%

Self-Monitoring of Blood Glucose (SMBG):
- Fasting: 80-130 mg/dL
- 1-2h postprandial: < 180 mg/dL
- Bedtime: 90-150 mg/dL

SECTION 3: PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT
=============================================================

First-Line Therapy:
METFORMIN (if eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73m²):
- Starting dose: 500 mg daily or 500 mg BID with meals
- Titrate every 1-2 weeks by 500 mg
- Target dose: 1000 mg BID (maximum 2550 mg/day)
- Hold: 48h before contrast administration (if eGFR < 60)
- Hold: If hospitalized, NPO, or critical illness
- Monitor: B12 levels annually (causes malabsorption)
- Benefit: Weight neutral, low hypoglycemia risk, reduces CV events (UKPDS)

Second-Line Agents — Selection Based on Comorbidities:

1. GLP-1 RECEPTOR AGONISTS (preferred if ASCVD, obesity, or T2D A1C far above goal):
   - Semaglutide (Ozempic): 0.5-2 mg SC weekly; or oral 7-14 mg daily
   - Liraglutide (Victoza): 0.6-1.8 mg SC daily
   - Dulaglutide (Trulicity): 0.75-4.5 mg SC weekly
   - Benefits: Weight loss (3-5 kg), CV risk reduction (LEADER, SUSTAIN-6, REWIND)
   - Contraindication: Personal/family history of MTC, MEN2
   - Monitor: GI side effects (nausea, vomiting), pancreatitis, thyroid nodules

2. SGLT-2 INHIBITORS (preferred if HF, CKD, or established ASCVD):
   - Empagliflozin (Jardiance): 10-25 mg daily (with breakfast)
   - Dapagliflozin (Farxiga): 5-10 mg daily
   - Canagliflozin (Invokana): 100-300 mg daily
   - Benefits: HF hospitalization reduction, CKD progression reduction, weight loss
   - Contraindication: eGFR < 20 (empagliflozin), < 25 (dapagliflozin), < 30 (canagliflozin)
   - Monitor: UTI, genital mycotic infections, DKA (rare), Fournier gangrene
   - Withhold before surgery (48-72h due to euglycemic DKA risk)

3. DPP-4 INHIBITORS (preferred if CKD without HF, elderly, low hypoglycemia risk needed):
   - Sitagliptin (Januvia): 100 mg daily (dose-adjust for eGFR < 45)
   - Saxagliptin (Onglyza): 2.5-5 mg daily (AVOID in HF — SAVOR-TIMI)
   - Linagliptin (Tradjenta): 5 mg daily (no renal dose adjustment needed)
   - Weight neutral, low hypoglycemia, modest A1C lowering (~0.5-0.8%)

4. INSULIN (when A1C significantly above goal or other agents contraindicated):
   - Basal insulin first: Glargine U-100/U-300, Detemir, Degludec
   - Start: 10 units SC at bedtime OR 0.1-0.2 units/kg/day
   - Titrate: Increase by 2 units every 3 days targeting fasting glucose 80-130 mg/dL
   - Add prandial insulin when A1C remains > 8% on optimized basal

5. SULFONYLUREAS (low cost but higher hypoglycemia risk):
   - Glipizide XL 5-20 mg daily (preferred in elderly — shorter acting)
   - Glimepiride 1-8 mg daily
   - Glyburide — AVOID in elderly (long half-life, highest hypoglycemia risk)

=============================================================
SECTION 4: HYPOGLYCEMIA MANAGEMENT
=============================================================

Hypoglycemia Definitions:
- Level 1 (Alert value): BG < 70 mg/dL — requires treatment
- Level 2 (Clinically significant): BG < 54 mg/dL — requires urgent treatment
- Level 3 (Severe): Altered consciousness/seizure requiring third-party assistance

Treatment — Rule of 15:
1. If conscious: 15g fast-acting carbohydrates
   - 4 glucose tablets OR 4 oz (120 mL) orange juice OR 6-8 regular gummy candies
2. Recheck BG in 15 minutes
3. Repeat if still < 70 mg/dL
4. Once BG > 70 mg/dL: Eat a small snack if next meal > 1 hour

Severe Hypoglycemia (unconscious/unable to swallow):
- Glucagon 1 mg IM/SC or intranasal (Baqsimi 3 mg)
- Call emergency services
- If IV access: Dextrose 25g (50 mL D50W) IV push

Prevention:
- Avoid skipping meals while on sulfonylurea or insulin
- Reduce insulin/sulfonylurea dose before unplanned exercise
- Adjust for alcohol consumption (inhibits hepatic gluconeogenesis)
- Annual hypoglycemia education and glucagon prescription for high-risk patients

=============================================================
SECTION 5: COMPLICATION SCREENING
=============================================================

Annual Screening (all T2D patients):
- Diabetic Retinopathy: Dilated eye exam (ophthalmology)
- Diabetic Nephropathy: Urine ACR + eGFR
- Diabetic Neuropathy: Monofilament foot exam, vibration testing
- ASCVD Risk: Lipid panel, BP measurement
- Thyroid: TSH (T1D and symptomatic)

Foot Care:
- Comprehensive foot exam at every visit (inspection, monofilament, pulses)
- Annual: ABI (Ankle-Brachial Index) if symptoms of PAD
- Podiatry referral: Active ulcer, Charcot foot, prior amputation
- Patient education: Daily foot inspection, proper footwear, no barefoot walking

Dental Care:
- Every 6 months (higher risk of periodontal disease)

=============================================================
SECTION 6: REFERRALS AND CARE TEAM
=============================================================

Endocrinology Referral Criteria:
- A1C > 9% despite multiple agents
- Frequent hypoglycemia or hypoglycemia unawareness
- Complex insulin regimens
- T1D management
- Pregnancy with diabetes
- Suspected MODY or other monogenic diabetes

MNT (Medical Nutrition Therapy):
- Refer to registered dietitian (RD) at diagnosis and annually
- No single "diabetic diet" — individualize carbohydrate intake
- Mediterranean or low-carbohydrate patterns associated with best outcomes

DSMES (Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support):
- At diagnosis, annually, and at transitions of care
- Proven to reduce A1C by 0.6-1.0% and improve quality of life
