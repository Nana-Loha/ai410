HYPERTENSION FOLLOW-UP AND MANAGEMENT GUIDELINE
Source: ACC/AHA 2017 High Blood Pressure Guidelines (DOI: 10.1161/HYP.0000000000000065),
        JNC 8 Panel Guidelines (JAMA 2014;311(5):507-520),
        ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes 2023
Last Updated: 2023

=============================================================
SECTION 1: CLASSIFICATION AND DEFINITIONS
=============================================================

Blood Pressure Categories (ACC/AHA 2017):
- Normal: SBP < 120 AND DBP < 80 mmHg
- Elevated: SBP 120-129 AND DBP < 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: SBP 130-139 OR DBP 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: SBP ≥ 140 OR DBP ≥ 90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: SBP > 180 AND/OR DBP > 120 mmHg

Hypertensive Crisis Subcategories:
- Urgency: SBP > 180/120 WITHOUT acute end-organ damage → reduce BP over 24-48h
- Emergency: SBP > 180/120 WITH acute end-organ damage → immediate IV treatment

End-Organ Damage Markers:
- Neurological: Hypertensive encephalopathy, stroke, TIA
- Cardiac: Acute MI, decompensated HF, aortic dissection
- Renal: AKI (creatinine rise > 0.5 mg/dL), hematuria
- Ophthalmological: Grade III-IV retinopathy, papilledema
- Vascular: Acute aortic dissection

=============================================================
SECTION 2: INITIAL EVALUATION
=============================================================

Confirm Diagnosis (Avoid White Coat Hypertension):
- Confirm with ≥ 2 readings on ≥ 2 separate occasions
- Home BP monitoring (HBPM): 7 days AM and PM, average last 6 days
- Ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM): 24h mean ≥ 130/80 confirms hypertension
- White coat hypertension: clinic BP elevated, home BP normal (ABPM < 130/80)

Baseline Laboratory Workup:
- Fasting lipid panel
- Fasting glucose / HbA1c
- Serum creatinine + eGFR
- Serum potassium and sodium
- Urinalysis with microscopy (protein, blood)
- Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR)
- 12-lead ECG (LVH detection)
- Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)

Secondary Hypertension Workup (if suspected):
- Renal artery stenosis: CT angiography, renal Doppler
- Primary aldosteronism: Plasma aldosterone/renin ratio (ARR > 30 + PA > 15 ng/dL)
- Pheochromocytoma: 24h urine catecholamines or plasma metanephrines
- Obstructive sleep apnea: Epworth Sleepiness Scale, polysomnography
- Cushing syndrome: 24h urine cortisol, overnight dexamethasone suppression test

Cardiovascular Risk Assessment:
- 10-year ASCVD risk: pooled cohort equations (≥ 10% = high risk)
- Global CVD risk determines treatment threshold and intensity

=============================================================
SECTION 3: TREATMENT TARGETS
=============================================================

BP Targets by Patient Population:
- General adult (no comorbidities): < 130/80 mmHg (ACC/AHA 2017)
- Age ≥ 65, community-dwelling: < 130 mmHg SBP (SPRINT trial)
- Diabetes mellitus: < 130/80 mmHg (ADA 2023)
- CKD (with proteinuria ACR > 300): < 130/80 mmHg
- CKD (without proteinuria): < 130/80 mmHg
- Heart failure with reduced EF (HFrEF): < 130/80 mmHg
- Post-stroke / TIA: < 130/80 mmHg
- Pregnancy: SBP 110-135 / DBP 85 mmHg

=============================================================
SECTION 4: PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT
=============================================================

First-Line Antihypertensive Agents:

1. THIAZIDE DIURETICS (preferred for uncomplicated hypertension):
   - Chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg daily (preferred over HCTZ per ALLHAT)
   - Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) 12.5-25 mg daily
   - Indapamide 1.25-2.5 mg daily
   - Monitor: Potassium, sodium, glucose, uric acid

2. ACE INHIBITORS (preferred in diabetes, CKD, HF, post-MI):
   - Lisinopril 5-40 mg daily
   - Ramipril 2.5-10 mg daily
   - Enalapril 5-40 mg daily (BID dosing)
   - Contraindicated: Pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, angioedema history
   - Monitor: Potassium, creatinine (within 1-2 weeks of initiation)

3. ARBs (alternative to ACEi, preferred if ACEi-intolerant):
   - Losartan 25-100 mg daily
   - Valsartan 80-320 mg daily
   - Same contraindications as ACEi; less cough
   - Do NOT combine ACEi + ARB (dual RAAS blockade harmful per ONTARGET)

4. CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS (preferred in elderly, isolated systolic HTN):
   - Amlodipine 2.5-10 mg daily (dihydropyridine — vasodilatory)
   - Diltiazem CD 120-540 mg daily (non-DHP — rate-lowering)
   - Verapamil SR 120-480 mg daily (non-DHP)
   - Avoid non-DHP CCBs with beta-blockers (heart block risk)

Compelling Indications — Preferred Drug Classes:
- Diabetes + HTN: ACEi or ARB (renal protection)
- CKD + Proteinuria: ACEi or ARB (reduces proteinuria)
- Post-MI: ACEi + Beta-blocker
- HFrEF: ACEi/ARB + Beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i
- Atrial fibrillation: Beta-blocker or non-DHP CCB (rate control)
- Recurrent stroke prevention: ACEi + thiazide

Initiation Strategy:
- Stage 1 (BP 130-139/80-89): Lifestyle modification first; add drug if 10-year ASCVD ≥ 10%
- Stage 2 (BP ≥ 140/90): Initiate drug therapy + lifestyle
- BP ≥ 160/100: Start with 2-drug combination therapy (ACEi/ARB + CCB preferred)

=============================================================
SECTION 5: FOLLOW-UP SCHEDULE
=============================================================

After Initiation or Dose Change:
- Week 2-4: BP recheck + side effect review + metabolic labs (potassium, creatinine)
- Month 3: Assess BP target achievement; adjust regimen as needed
- Month 6: If BP at goal, may extend interval to every 6 months

Stable, Controlled Hypertension:
- Every 3-6 months: BP measurement
- Annual: Fasting lipids, glucose/HbA1c, creatinine, potassium, urinalysis
- Every 1-2 years: ECG if LVH present at baseline
- Ophthalmology referral: If diabetic or Grade III-IV retinopathy

Uncontrolled Hypertension Workup:
1. Confirm adherence (pill count, refill history, MEMS cap)
2. Assess white coat effect (home BP monitoring)
3. Evaluate for interfering substances (NSAIDs, oral contraceptives, decongestants, 
   stimulants, licorice, excessive alcohol, cocaine)
4. Consider secondary hypertension workup
5. Intensify lifestyle interventions
6. Add or uptitrate antihypertensive agent

Referral Criteria (to Hypertension Specialist):
- Resistant hypertension (BP uncontrolled on 3 agents including diuretic)
- Suspected secondary hypertension
- Hypertensive crisis with end-organ damage
- Complex comorbidities (CKD stage 4-5, transplant, pregnancy)

=============================================================
SECTION 6: LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS
=============================================================

DASH Diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension):
- Rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy
- Low in saturated fat, red meat, sweets
- Expected BP reduction: 8-14 mmHg SBP

Sodium Restriction:
- Target: < 2.3 g/day (100 mmol/day) — ideally < 1.5 g/day
- Expected BP reduction: 2-8 mmHg SBP

Physical Activity:
- Aerobic: 150 min/week moderate-intensity (brisk walking, cycling, swimming)
- Resistance training: 2-3 sessions/week
- Expected BP reduction: 4-9 mmHg SBP

Weight Reduction:
- 1 mmHg reduction per kg lost
- Target BMI < 25 kg/m²

Alcohol Limitation:
- Men: ≤ 2 drinks/day; Women: ≤ 1 drink/day
- Expected BP reduction: 2-4 mmHg SBP

Smoking Cessation:
- Does not directly lower BP but substantially reduces cardiovascular risk

=============================================================
SECTION 7: SPECIAL POPULATIONS
=============================================================

Hypertension in Elderly (Age ≥ 65):
- Start low, go slow — initiate at lowest dose
- Orthostatic hypotension screening at every visit (sit-to-stand BP)
- Fall risk assessment before intensifying therapy
- Avoid alpha-blockers as monotherapy (orthostatic hypotension)
- SPRINT trial: SBP < 120 reduces CV events but increases falls and AKI

Hypertension in Pregnancy:
- Chronic HTN: Treat if SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 (CHIPS trial)
- Safe medications: Methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine
- AVOID: ACEi, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors (teratogenic)
- Preeclampsia: SBP ≥ 160 or DBP ≥ 110 with proteinuria → hospitalize
