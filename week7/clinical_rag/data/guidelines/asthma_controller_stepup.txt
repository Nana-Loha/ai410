ASTHMA CONTROLLER STEP-UP THERAPY GUIDELINE
Source: GINA (Global Initiative for Asthma) Report 2023 (www.ginasthma.org),
        NAEPP EPR-3 Guidelines (NIH Publication No. 08-4051),
        ATS/ERS Task Force on Severe Asthma 2014
Last Updated: 2023

=============================================================
SECTION 1: ASTHMA DIAGNOSIS AND CLASSIFICATION
=============================================================

Diagnostic Criteria:
1. History of variable respiratory symptoms (wheeze, shortness of breath, 
   chest tightness, cough — often worse at night/early morning)
2. Variable expiratory airflow limitation confirmed by:
   - Spirometry: FEV1/FVC < 0.70 (post-bronchodilator)
   - Positive bronchodilator reversibility: FEV1 increase ≥ 12% AND ≥ 200 mL after 
     400 mcg albuterol (salbutamol)
   - Positive bronchial provocation test (methacholine PC20 < 8 mg/mL)
   - Peak flow variability > 10% diurnal or > 20% between visits

Asthma Severity Classification (before treatment):
- Intermittent: Symptoms ≤ 2 days/week, nighttime ≤ 2x/month, FEV1 ≥ 80%
- Mild Persistent: Symptoms > 2 days/week but not daily, FEV1 ≥ 80%
- Moderate Persistent: Daily symptoms, nighttime > 1x/week, FEV1 60-80%
- Severe Persistent: Continuous symptoms, frequent nighttime, FEV1 < 60%

Asthma Control Categories (during treatment):
Well-Controlled (ALL criteria):
- Daytime symptoms ≤ 2 days/week
- Nighttime awakening ≤ 2x/month
- SABA use ≤ 2 days/week (not for EIB prevention)
- No activity limitation
- ACQ-5 score ≤ 0.75; ACT score ≥ 20

Not Well-Controlled (any of):
- Daytime symptoms > 2 days/week
- Nighttime awakening > 2x/month
- SABA use > 2 days/week
- Some activity limitation
- ACQ-5 score 0.75-1.5; ACT score 16-19

Very Poorly Controlled:
- Symptoms throughout the day
- Nighttime awakening ≥ 4x/week
- SABA use several times/day
- Extreme activity limitation
- ACQ-5 > 1.5; ACT score ≤ 15

=============================================================
SECTION 2: STEPWISE TREATMENT APPROACH
=============================================================

STEP 1 — Intermittent Asthma:
Preferred: As-needed (PRN) low-dose ICS-formoterol (GINA 2023 preferred track)
  OR PRN SABA alone (if ICS-formoterol unavailable)
Alternative: Low-dose ICS whenever SABA is used

Medications:
- Albuterol (salbutamol) MDI 90 mcg/puff: 1-2 puffs PRN (max 8 puffs/day)
- Budesonide-formoterol 80/4.5 mcg: 1 puff PRN (SMART therapy)

STEP 2 — Mild Persistent Asthma:
Preferred: Daily low-dose ICS + PRN SABA OR PRN low-dose ICS-formoterol
Alternative: LTRA (montelukast) + PRN SABA; theophylline + PRN SABA (less preferred)

Low-dose ICS Options:
- Fluticasone propionate MDI 44 mcg: 1-2 puffs BID
- Budesonide DPI 180 mcg: 1 puff BID
- Beclomethasone dipropionate 40-80 mcg: 1-2 puffs BID
- Ciclesonide 80 mcg: 1 puff daily (once-daily dosing advantage)

LTRA (if ICS not tolerated or refused):
- Montelukast 10 mg PO daily (evening; FDA black box warning for neuropsychiatric effects)

STEP 3 — Moderate Persistent Asthma:
Preferred: Low-dose ICS-LABA combination daily + PRN SABA
            OR Medium-dose ICS daily + PRN SABA

ICS-LABA Combinations:
- Fluticasone-salmeterol (Advair) 100/50 mcg DPI: 1 puff BID
- Budesonide-formoterol (Symbicort) 80/4.5 mcg: 1-2 puffs BID
- Fluticasone-vilanterol (Breo Ellipta) 100/25 mcg: 1 puff daily
- Mometasone-formoterol (Dulera) 100/5 mcg: 2 puffs BID

LABA SAFETY WARNING:
- LABA must ALWAYS be used WITH ICS (never as monotherapy in asthma)
- LABA monotherapy in asthma associated with increased asthma-related death

Medium-dose ICS (Step 3 alternative):
- Fluticasone propionate MDI 110-220 mcg: 2 puffs BID
- Budesonide DPI 360 mcg: 2 puffs BID

STEP 4 — Moderate-to-Severe Persistent Asthma:
Preferred: Medium-to-high dose ICS-LABA + PRN SABA
Alternative: Medium-dose ICS + LTRA or theophylline

High-dose ICS-LABA:
- Fluticasone-salmeterol 250/50 mcg or 500/50 mcg DPI: 1 puff BID
- Budesonide-formoterol 160/4.5 mcg: 2 puffs BID
- Consider add-on LAMA: Tiotropium (Spiriva Respimat) 2.5 mcg: 2 puffs daily

STEP 5 — Severe Persistent Asthma:
Preferred: High-dose ICS-LABA + specialist referral for add-on therapy

Add-on Biologic Options (require specialist evaluation):
1. Anti-IgE: Omalizumab (Xolair) — for allergic asthma, IgE 30-700 IU/mL
   Dose: 150-375 mg SC every 2-4 weeks based on IgE and weight
   
2. Anti-IL-5: Mepolizumab (Nucala) 100 mg SC q4 weeks — eosinophils ≥ 150 cells/mcL
   Benralizumab (Fasenra) 30 mg SC q4 weeks x3, then q8 weeks
   
3. Anti-IL-4/IL-13: Dupilumab (Dupixent) 200-300 mg SC q2 weeks — type 2 inflammation
   
4. Anti-TSLP: Tezepelumab (Tezspire) 210 mg SC q4 weeks — broadest eligibility

Oral Corticosteroids (last resort, step 5):
- Prednisone 7.5-40 mg daily (lowest effective dose)
- Minimize systemic steroid exposure (osteoporosis, HPA suppression, cataracts)
- Always attempt steroid-sparing strategies before chronic OCS

=============================================================
SECTION 3: WHEN TO STEP UP THERAPY
=============================================================

Indications to Step Up (after confirming):
1. Confirm poor control is NOT due to:
   - Poor inhaler technique (most common reason for treatment failure)
   - Non-adherence to prescribed regimen
   - Ongoing allergen or irritant exposure (pets, mold, smoke)
   - Comorbidities worsening control (GERD, rhinitis, obesity, anxiety)
   - Incorrect diagnosis (VCD, cardiac dyspnea, hyperventilation)

2. If above confirmed, step up therapy:
   - Not Well-Controlled: Step up 1 level; reassess in 2-6 weeks
   - Very Poorly Controlled: Consider short oral steroid burst + step up 1-2 levels
   - Worsening exacerbation: Oral steroid burst, reassess triggers, step up

Step-Up Decision Algorithm:
[ACT score < 20 OR ACQ > 0.75] → Verify technique and adherence → 
If adherent + correct technique → Step up therapy

Short-Term Oral Steroid Burst (for acute worsening):
- Prednisone 40-60 mg/day x 5-7 days (adults)
- No taper needed for courses < 2 weeks
- Triggers for burst: ACT < 15, nocturnal symptoms most nights, ER visit

=============================================================
SECTION 4: ACUTE EXACERBATION MANAGEMENT
=============================================================

Mild-Moderate Exacerbation (home management):
- Albuterol 2-4 puffs every 20 minutes x 3 doses, then every 1-4 hours PRN
- If response incomplete after 1 hour: Oral prednisone 40-60 mg
- Reassess in 24-48 hours

Severe Exacerbation (Emergency Department):
- Albuterol 2.5 mg nebulized every 20 minutes x 3 (or MDI 4-8 puffs with spacer)
- Ipratropium 0.5 mg nebulized with each albuterol treatment x 3 (first hour only)
- Systemic corticosteroid: Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV OR Prednisone 40 mg PO
- Oxygen: Titrate SpO2 ≥ 93%
- Peak flow or FEV1: Assess before and after bronchodilator

Admission Criteria:
- FEV1 or PEF < 40% predicted after treatment
- SpO2 < 92% on room air
- Persistent severe symptoms despite aggressive treatment
- High-risk features: prior intubation, ICU admission, ≥ 2 hospitalizations/year

Life-Threatening Asthma (ICU):
- Heliox-driven albuterol nebulization
- IV magnesium sulfate 2g over 20 minutes (bronchodilator effect)
- Non-invasive positive pressure ventilation (if no contraindication)
- Intubation if respiratory failure

=============================================================
SECTION 5: MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP
=============================================================

Routine Follow-Up:
- Newly initiated therapy: 2-4 weeks
- After step-up: 4-6 weeks
- Controlled asthma: Every 3-6 months
- Annual: Spirometry to reassess lung function trajectory

Inhaler Technique Review (Every Visit):
- Demonstrate technique at every visit
- Use spacer/valved holding chamber with MDI (doubles lung deposition)
- Check: inhaler not empty (dose counter), coordination of actuation-inhalation

Step-Down Considerations:
- When well-controlled for ≥ 3 months
- Step down 1 level at a time
- Monitor closely for loss of control
- Complete step-down to lowest effective dose

Referral to Pulmonology/Allergy:
- Step 3-4 not achieving control
- Frequent exacerbations (≥ 2 OCS courses/year)
- Suspected occupational asthma
- Biologics evaluation (Step 5)
- Difficult-to-treat or severe asthma workup
